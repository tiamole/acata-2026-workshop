3. Item Review Rubric
A structured rubric for validity-oriented review, alignment, language quality, accessibility, fairness, and technical item quality.
Purpose
Use this rubric during expert review of drafted items. It supports systematic judgment before pilot testing. It does not substitute for validation evidence based on response processes, internal structure, relations to other variables, consequences, or psychometric analyses when those forms of evidence are required.

Fairness and accessibility review prompts
Would a learner need cultural or regional knowledge that is not part of the construct?
Would unfamiliar names, settings, occupations, technology, or socioeconomic assumptions affect performance?
Does the item require more reading, vision, hearing, motor interaction, or working memory than the construct requires?
Could simplification improve access without lowering the intended cognitive demand?
Is the context sensitive, stigmatizing, or emotionally loaded without a measurement reason?
If a concern is plausible but uncertain, should it be flagged for cognitive interviewing, pilot analysis, or differential item functioning analysis rather than resolved by guesswork?

Inter-reviewer use
Recommended practice
When two or more reviewers are used, have them rate independently before discussion. Compare category-level differences, resolve disagreements by evidence, and retain the original ratings when possible. Agreement statistics can support quality monitoring, but numerical agreement does not replace substantive adjudication.
